

**The angle:** Every fact in this carousel is something women commonly aren't taught — not because it's obscure, but because sex ed, media, and even friend-group conversation consistently leave it out. Naming that gap directly ("nobody taught you") does two things at once: it's inherently shareable (people share things that validate "wait, me too, nobody told me that either") and it puts Nancy on the right side of a real cultural frustration, not just selling a product.

## Why it's effective

1. **It's the highest-share content type in this category.** Corrective, "here's what they left out" education outperforms almost everything else in wellness because sharing it signals the sharer is informed — not just endorsing a brand. This is a save-and-send format by design.

2. **It's censorship-proof.** Sexual wellness brands get shadowbanned and ad-restricted constantly. This carousel teaches through anatomy facts and plain language — no flagged words, no explicit imagery — so it can actually run and reach people, unlike a lot of what competitors try to post.

3. **It builds trust before it sells.** Five of the eight slides teach something with zero mention of a product. That's deliberate — the "education first, product second" ratio keeps reach healthy and makes Nancy read as a credible voice, not just an ad account. The two product moments (Lem, Berri) appear only where they illustrate the fact being taught.

4. **It directly serves the brand mission.** "Break Taboo, Embrace Pleasure" isn't just a tagline here — this carousel *is* the taboo-breaking, in a format people will screenshot and pass along.

5. **It converts curiosity into a list.** The comment-START mechanic turns passive readers into a warm, owned lead list (email via DM), which is worth more long-term than likes — especially in a category where the paid-ad channel can get shut off with no warning.

**Predicted performance:** optimize for saves and shares over reach — that's the metric this format is built to win, and it's the one that compounds (an account's average reach follows its save-rate over time, not the other way around).

**Segments:** Primary — First-Timers (18–27, knowledge/shame gap). Secondary — Reconnectors (28–40) and Reclaimers (45–60), for whom the responsive-desire and sensation-pattern slides land hardest.
