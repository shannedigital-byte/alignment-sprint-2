## Why it's written this way

- **The graphic only asks the question.** The caption has to deliver the answer, so the first line resolves the headline immediately — no swipe, no link, no wait.
- **Plain-language mechanism.** The middle paragraph explains *why* without jargon: the stimulation that works is happening in foreplay, then it stops.
- **"Not chemistry. Not you. Just the order."** Three short declaratives — Nancy states, she doesn't cushion. This is the line that removes the self-blame.
- **Open CTA.** "Tell us about your experience" invites a real reply instead of a one-word code, which is what actually drives comment volume on a question-led post.

## Voice-guideline check

- **Straightforward, not technical:** no clinical jargon
- **Direct, not explicit:** describes the mechanism without explicit language
- **Risqué, not pornographic:** implies rather than states
- **Playful, not childish:** dry and flat, no cutesy tone
- **States, doesn't ask:** the only question mark lives on the graphic, not in the body copy
- **Moderation-safe:** uses "external stimulation" rather than the anatomical term — clearer to a general reader and safer against Meta/TikTok filters, consistent with Posts 1 and 2
